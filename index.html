WatchIPTV — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 10 August 2026

WatchIPTV is a player for video streams you already have: RTSP cameras on your own network, HLS and other links, and IPTV playlists in .m3u form. It ships with no channels, no playlist and no accounts of its own. Everything it plays is something you add.

The short version. WatchIPTV has no server. It collects nothing, sends nothing to us, and contains no analytics, no advertising and no tracking of any kind. Your playlists, addresses and passwords are stored encrypted on your own device and are never transmitted anywhere except to the stream you asked it to open — and, if you pair a watch, to your own watch.

What the app stores, and where

  • Your streams. The name, address, folder and options for each stream you add or import. Stream addresses often contain a username and password, so this file is written encrypted, with a key held in the device's hardware-backed keystore. It never leaves the device except as described under “Sending a playlist to your watch”, below.
  • Your settings. Preferences such as which streams appear on the watch, whether to prefer the SD sub-stream, and which stream each watch tile shows.
  • Still pictures. When you watch a stream, the app may keep one still frame from it so the home screen and the watch tile can show what the stream looks like instead of a black tile. These images are stored in the app's private storage and are never uploaded. You can turn the whole feature off in Settings, which also stops the app connecting to anything you have not opened yourself.
  • A diagnostics log. A rolling technical log — what the app tried to play, which transport it used, what the error was — kept in the app's private storage so that a problem can be described accurately. It is never sent automatically. It leaves the device only if you tap “Send diagnostics” yourself and choose where to send it. Passwords are removed before anything is written — every line, including messages from the video player and crash stack traces, is filtered so a stream address is recorded as rtsp://user:••••••@192.168.1.180/stream1. A log does still contain the host names, ports, paths and usernames of your streams, so send it to someone you would show that to.

All of this lives in the app's own private storage. Uninstalling the app removes it. Nothing is backed up to Google Drive or transferred to a new device: automatic backup and device-to-device transfer are switched off deliberately, so your stream passwords cannot leave your device that way.

Network connections the app makes

Only the ones you ask for. WatchIPTV connects to:

  • the stream you open, or whose still picture you asked it to refresh;
  • the playlist URL you enter, when you import or refresh a playlist;
  • your paired watch, over Google's Wearable Data Layer, if you use the watch app.

There is no other destination. The app contacts no server operated by us, because there isn't one.

Sending a playlist to your watch

If you use the Wear OS app, the streams you mark for the watch are sent from your phone to your own watch over Google's Wearable Data Layer, which is the standard Android mechanism for phone-to-watch communication and is handled by Google Play services on your own devices. That transfer includes the stream addresses, and therefore any credentials inside them, because the watch has to be able to open the stream. On the watch they are stored encrypted, the same way as on the phone. Only streams you have marked for the watch are sent.

Permissions, and why each one is needed

  • Internet and network state — to open the streams and playlists you add.
  • Wi-Fi state and Wi-Fi lock — a camera on your home network is only reachable over Wi-Fi, so the app can ask the device to bring Wi-Fi up and hold it while a local stream plays. This is used to route the connection, not to determine your location.
  • Notifications — to show what is playing, with pause and stop controls, while a stream is open on the phone, and on the watch so a stream your phone sends can be opened when the screen is off.
  • Foreground service, media playback — so the stream is not cut off by the system while it is playing, and so sound carries on when the app is off screen if you tap the keep-playing button. On the phone that means switching to another app; on the watch, the screen turning off.

That is the complete list: INTERNET, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE, ACCESS_WIFI_STATE, CHANGE_WIFI_STATE, CHANGE_NETWORK_STATE, WAKE_LOCK, POST_NOTIFICATIONS, FOREGROUND_SERVICE and FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK.

The app asks for no location permission, no contacts, no microphone, no camera hardware, no storage outside its own folder, no full-screen-intent permission, and no advertising identifier.

Children

WatchIPTV is a general-purpose media player. It is not directed at children, and it collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Content

WatchIPTV provides no content. It does not host, index, supply, recommend or search for streams, and it comes with none built in. It plays addresses that you enter or import, and you are responsible for having the right to access them.

Third parties

The app contains no analytics SDK, no advertising SDK, no crash-reporting service and no social SDK. Its libraries — AndroidX, Jetpack Compose, and Media3/ExoPlayer for playback — run entirely on your device and send nothing anywhere. Google Play services is used only for the phone-to-watch link described above.

Your control

  • Delete any stream, or clear the whole library, from within the app.
  • Turn off still pictures in Settings, which stops all background connections.
  • Clear the diagnostics log from Settings.
  • Uninstall the app, which deletes everything it stored.

Because nothing is collected and nothing is transmitted to us, there is no data of yours for us to show you, export or delete on request — there is none to hold.

Changes to this policy

If the app ever starts collecting or transmitting anything, this page will say so, with a new date at the top, before that version is released.
